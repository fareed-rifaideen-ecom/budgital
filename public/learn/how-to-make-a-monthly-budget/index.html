Budgeting guide

How to make a monthly budget

Short answer: list the income you expect, give each major expense category a realistic plan, record actual spending, and review the difference before the next month starts.

1. Start with take-home income

Use money you expect to receive during the month, not a best-case estimate. If your income changes, start with a conservative baseline and treat extra income as a separate decision.

2. Separate fixed, flexible, and irregular costs

Fixed costs include rent, subscriptions, and scheduled payments. Flexible costs include groceries, dining, and transport. Irregular costs—such as annual renewals, travel, or repairs—need a monthly sinking-fund amount so they do not surprise you.

3. Set a category plan, then track actuals

A budget is useful when it shows both the plan and the actual. Keep the category list small enough to maintain, and adjust it when real spending teaches you something. The goal is a more accurate next month, not a perfect first month.

4. Review what is left

Subtract spending from income to see the amount still unallocated. Then decide whether it should stay as a buffer, move toward a goal, or receive a new category plan.

Common questions

Do I need to link my bank? No. Manual entry and CSV import can be enough for a private monthly budget, especially when you want control over which data leaves your accounts.

Should I use zero-based budgeting? It is one option. Some people prefer a flexible category plan or a 50/30/20 starting point. Choose the method that you can repeat.
